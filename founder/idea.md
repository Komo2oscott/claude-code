# DIRTY is Better ("Dirty Greens")

- What it is: field-packed, unwashed leafy greens sold in grocery stores with a mesh wash bag in the container, so the customer washes them at home instead of buying chlorine-washed bagged salad.
- Who it is for: grocery shoppers who buy bagged salad greens and want fresher-tasting, less processed produce (the brand's tone, merch and TikTok/Instagram presence point to younger, style-conscious shoppers). Not yet specified by the founder.
- What it sells, at what price: four greens, Sir Leafs-A-Lot Arugula, Rosie's Iron Will Spinach, Kali-fornia Baby Kale, Astro Greens Mixed Leaf. Retail price per container: UNKNOWN (not listed on the site; greens are sold in store only). Merch sold online: tee $35, trucker hat $32, tote $30, bandanas $20, poster set $15, koozies $10, air freshener $8.
- Where and how: live and in retail; sold in grocery stores ("Shop in store", "Find in a store near you"), including at least Gelson's (quoted on the site). Instagram and TikTok for awareness. Grown/packed by "Farmer Tim", described as having decades of growing experience. Store count and region beyond that: UNKNOWN.
- Budget and constraints: tried to raise $1M (to reach 60 stores, which the founder says turns a profit); the raise did not succeed. Current cash and runway: UNKNOWN.
- Pilot results (founder, 2026-11-21): 7 Gelson's stores in LA for 11 weeks; strong sales and repeat sales. Retail $6.99, 35% to the store (about $4.54 to DIRTY per container). Lost $2-$4 per container (implied all-in cost about $6.50-$8.50). Longer shelf life and no chemicals were the main selling points, especially during a cyclospora scare involving Taylor Farms. Food safety is built in (founder: "totally solid").
- The decision now: (A) raise or finance processing machinery to cut unit cost and scale greens, or (B) keep the brand and pivot to shelf-stable products (salsa, prepared meals). The founder sees the brand as the main asset.

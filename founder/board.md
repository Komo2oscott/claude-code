# Board summary: DIRTY is Better

**Vote: 3 FUND IF (Offers 4, Monopoly 4, Product 5). Average score: 4.3 / 10.**

All three members would fund it, but only once the conditions below are met. The memos are in `founder/board/` (offers.md, monopoly.md, product.md).

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **Food safety and regulation (all three).** Selling unwashed greens that look ready to eat puts safety in the customer's sink. One outbreak, a recall or a retailer pulling the product would end the brand. Monopoly adds that it could end the whole category. Status: UNKNOWN.
2. **The wash step works against the promise (Offers, Product).** The tagline promises "grocery store convenience". The ritual is a 5-minute soak, a rinse, a shake and hanging the bag to dry, which is more work than the bagged salad it replaces. If the taste gain isn't obvious on the first bite, repeat purchases fall.
3. **No measured proof that it's better (all three).** Today the claims are taste, smell and "no chlorine". Shelf life against pre-washed greens, blind taste preference and in-store shrink are all UNKNOWN. With no number, there's nothing to defend (Monopoly) and nothing to promise (Offers).
4. **Easy to copy and a scale business (Monopoly; Offers asks who shares the shelf).** Leaving out a wash step isn't proprietary. A large grower or a store's private label could launch an unwashed line with more acres and lower unit costs.

## Where they disagreed

- **The brand and merch.** Offers and Monopoly count the name, voice and merch as strengths, and Monopoly calls the brand the only moat in view. Product says park the merch until the greens show repeat purchase, because it pulls focus from the one thing the business must do brilliantly.
- **The mesh bag.** Offers counts it as a useful part of the offer. Product says a new bag in every container turns into clutter for loyal buyers, and suggests selling the bag once and refills after that.
- **How to grow.** Monopoly wants it to win one regional premium chain before going wide. The other two set no limit on where it sells, only on proof.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] **A written food-safety and labelling position**, accepted by retailers and with a clear "wash before eating" on the front of the pack, before adding more stores. *(all three)*
- [ ] **Measured shelf life against pre-washed greens**, plus in-store shrink data from current stores. *(all three)* `/founder-ops`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **A blind taste-preference result against pre-washed greens**, printed on the pack if it wins. *(Offers, Monopoly)* `/founder-consumer`
- [ ] **Repeat-purchase data** from current stores showing buyers come back after their first wash. *(Offers, Product)* `/founder-consumer`
- [ ] **A shorter wash ritual**: tested with real customers and cut to about 2 minutes, with no bag to hang. *(Offers, Product)* `/founder-offer`
- [ ] **A guarantee the business can afford**, such as "fresh for X days or your money back". *(Offers)* `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **Unit economics**: retail price, wholesale price, cost of goods and margin. *(all three asked)* `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **Map who shares the shelf** (organic, hydroponic, living lettuce, private label) and what stops them launching an unwashed line. *(Offers, Monopoly)* `/founder-competitors`
- [ ] **Durability**: grower contracts or exclusive supply, a plan for out-of-season supply and for relying on one grower, and a trademark on the "DIRTY" category language. *(Monopoly)* `/founder-ops`
- [ ] **Win one niche first**, such as one regional premium grocer with high repeat purchase, before going wide. *(Monopoly)* `/founder-launch`
- [ ] **Decide on the bag**: either sell it once with greens refills after, or show why a bag in every container is worth the cost. *(Product)* `/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **Merch on hold** until the greens show repeat purchase. *(Product; Offers and Monopoly disagree)*

## The strongest version the board can see

This is not quite the business as pitched. The board sees a premium, proof-led greens brand that wins one premium regional grocer before anywhere else. It leads with a measured advantage ("lasts X days longer" or "chosen over pre-washed in blind tests") rather than the anti-chlorine story alone. The pack carries a food-safety label retailers sign off on, and the wash takes under two minutes. A reusable wash bag comes once, with refills after it, which makes the ritual a habit and gives buyers a reason to keep choosing DIRTY over a copycat. The brand voice stays, because it is the one thing a large grower can't easily copy. The merch waits until repeat purchase shows that people come back for the greens, not the attitude. If shelf-life or taste tests show no real advantage, the board's support falls away, and that is the first thing to find out.

# Board summary: DIRTY is Better (round 3)

**Vote: 3 FUND IF (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 5). Average score: 4.7 / 10, unchanged from round 2.**

**The direction moved. All three now accept that the brand, not only the leaf, is the asset.** Each recommends a *brand-led* business with the greens as its flagship and its proof: co-pack the greens to cut cost, use the characters to sell them, and extend the brand only after the greens make money on each container, and only into products where "DIRTY" still means something a buyer can check. None backs a shelf-stable *replacement*. Offers no longer rules out shelf-stable automatically. Product still rules out salsa specifically.

Memos: `founder/board/` (offers.md, monopoly.md, product.md). Earlier rounds: `round1/`, `round2/`.

## What the founder's pushback changed

| | Round 2 | Round 3 |
|---|---|---|
| The brand | Valuable only inside greens | A real asset that can span products (all three) |
| Machinery | Finance it once the cost case is proven | **A co-packer, not bought machinery**: a cost per unit, no capital, fits a failed raise (all three) |
| Marketing | Pause merch | Demos are a proven, cheap sales tool. Merch can stay if it pays for itself |
| Shelf-stable | No (all three) | Not as a replacement. A later extension only if it carries a true DIRTY claim |

## The numbers (all three checked them)

- **Packing:** 3 × $25 × 3 h = $225 per 200 containers = **$1.13 per container**. A 70% cut saves **about $0.79**.
- **Loss per container:** about $1.96–$3.96 before the saving, **about $1.17–$3.17 after it**. Packing is only about 13–17% of the $6.50–$8.50 all-in cost, and the saving closes only about 20–40% of the gap.
- **The number nobody has: what makes up the other ~$5.40–$7.40 per container.** All three name this the top item that would kill it. It decides whether the business works.
- **Demos:** $60 / 30 packs = **$2.00 a pack**. That is cheap for winning a customer. At today's costs, though, a pack sold at a demo loses about $3.96–$5.96 on the first sale. Demos pay off only through repeat purchases, and repeat purchases pay off only once each container makes money.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **The unexplained ~$5.40–$7.40 doesn't fall** with co-packing or scale. Then every marketing dollar buys more loss. *(all three)*
2. **Demo buyers don't come back**, or demand leaned on the cyclospora scare. Then the $2.00 per pack is never earned back. *(all three)*
3. **The brand spreads before the hero product works.** Characters, merch and new products eat cash the greens need. Or the brand ends up in aisles where "DIRTY" proves nothing and competes on vibe alone. *(all three)*
4. **Copycats.** A co-packer can pack an unwashed line for anyone, so co-packing cuts cost but builds no moat. The brand and a claim buyers can check have to be the moat. *(Monopoly; Offers asks the same)*

## Where they disagreed

- **How far the brand can stretch.** Offers would allow shelf-stable later if it carries a true "DIRTY" claim, such as no chemical wash or a short ingredient list. Monopoly allows any product with a "less processed" claim buyers can check, sold through the same co-packer or grocery buyer. Product limits extensions to *fresh*, minimally handled products and still rules out salsa, because it contradicts the brand's own comparison table.
- **Who the buyer is.** Monopoly flags a conflict. "Play outside like a kid" and the characters point toward families, while "FRESH AS F*CK" points away from them. Pick one small market first. The others didn't raise it.
- **Playful "dirt" after a food-safety scare.** Product warns that it could make some buyers and produce buyers wary. The others treat the scare as an opening.
- **Merch.** Offers and Monopoly see merch and the city tour as a way to *measure* whether people buy the brand for itself. Product keeps merch only if it breaks even.

## How to split scarce money (consensus)

1. **Unit cost first:** a co-packer quote and the full cost breakdown.
2. **Demos second:** a budgeted, measured test in the 7 Gelson's stores, kept only where demo buyers come back enough to pay back the $2.00.
3. **Paid social last**, once each container makes money.
4. **Financing:** co-packer terms, an equipment lease, retailer support, or debt secured on purchase orders, sized to the cost model. No new $1M equity raise.

## The strongest version the board can see

This is closer to the founder's vision than round 2 was. DIRTY is a fun, anti-processed food brand with a cast of characters. One line sums it up: "food that's still alive, for people who'd rather be outside." The greens are its flagship and its proof. They are co-packed, so the cost per container falls without buying machines, and priced at whatever level a test shows buyers will pay. Two hero characters sell them in demos that pay for themselves through repeat buyers. The wash ritual is the brand's signature "get dirty" moment. Merch and the city tour keep the world alive between purchases and *measure* whether people want DIRTY for its own sake. Once each container makes money, the brand earns the right to a second product with a true DIRTY claim of its own, and growth is paid for with debt against orders rather than equity. The board's one hard line is the order of things: the brand can be the asset, but it borrows its credibility from a leaf people can taste, so the greens have to stop losing money first. Everything depends on one number nobody has yet: what makes up the other ~$5.40–$7.40 per container.

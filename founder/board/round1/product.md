# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pitch fits in one sentence:** "unwashed leafy greens that you wash at home." The comparison table shows it at a glance.
- **It has a point of view.** "Dirt is proof of life" turns a missing step into the reason to buy. The brand looks deliberate across the packaging, site, merch and social channels.
- **It takes one experience back from the factory.** The wash ritual is the product, not an extra, and the reusable mesh bag shows someone thought about the moment of use.
- **It is already live.** It is on grocery shelves, and the site quotes a produce manager at a retail partner.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The product depends on a step the business does not control.** Safety now rests on the customer washing properly. A shopper who skips the soak, or eats from a container that looks ready to eat, is one bad outcome from ending the brand. The seam between shelf and sink is where this product breaks, and it is not designed yet. The food-safety and regulatory position is UNKNOWN.
2. **The experience contradicts the promise.** The tagline says "grocery store convenience". The ritual is a 5-minute soak, a rinse, a shake and hanging the bag to dry. Starting from the customer's Tuesday night, that is more effort than the product it replaces. If the ritual feels like a chore by the third purchase, people stop buying again. A mesh bag in every container also means bags pile up for loyal buyers, so the reuse story turns into clutter.
3. **No one owns the hidden parts.** Shelf life against pre-washed greens, how the cold chain treats unwashed leaves, and how "dirty" looks in a store's display case are all UNKNOWN. Wilted or gritty greens on a shelf will be read as poor quality, not as proof of life.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What does the first five minutes feel like at home, and have you watched real customers do it? Where did they hesitate?
- What happens when someone does not wash? What does the label say, and what is your liability position?
- Why does every container include a new mesh bag? Could you sell the bag once and the greens refill after that?
- What is the shelf life in days against pre-washed greens, and what is the shrink rate at the store? (No one has this number. /founder-ops and /founder-cfo would answer it.)
- What is the retail price, and does it beat the pre-washed bag next to it? (/founder-competitors and /founder-pricing would answer this.)
- Do repeat buyers keep doing the ritual? (/founder-consumer would answer this.)
- What would you cut? The merch line and the four product names both pull attention away from the one thing the business must do brilliantly: greens that taste clearly better.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **A documented food-safety position**, with clear wash-before-eating labelling on the front of the pack and the regulatory sign-off this category needs.
2. **Ritual testing with real customers**, watched in person or on video, and the steps cut until the wash takes 2 minutes or less with no bag to hang up.
3. **Shelf-life and shrink data from current stores**, showing the greens hold up in the display case as well as pre-washed ones.
4. **The bag offered once and refills after**, or a reason the bag in every container is worth its cost and clutter.
5. **Merch parked until the greens show repeat purchase.**

# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers have money and are easy to reach.** Bagged-greens shoppers are already standing in the produce aisle with a budget, and the product is live on grocery shelves. A store's produce manager welcoming it is an early sign of reach. Store count and sell-through are UNKNOWN.
- **The dream outcome is vivid.** "Farmers' market flavor, grocery store convenience" and greens that smell "like actual food" give the shopper a clear thing to want. It is not a cheaper version of something, so it is not competing on price.
- **The stack has started.** The reusable mesh wash bag deals with the obvious problem ("how do I wash this?") by turning it into a tool, and doubles as storage. The name, voice and merch make the offer easy to remember.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It asks the buyer to do more.** The value equation drops as effort and time go up, and this offer adds both: a 5-minute soak, a rinse, a hang to dry. People buy bagged salad to skip that work. Unless the flavor and freshness gain is felt on the first bite, the buyer reads it as paying for less service.
2. **Likelihood has no proof.** The only claims are taste and smell, plus a "chemical" comparison. There is no shelf-life number, no taste test and no guarantee. If the greens do not clearly last longer or taste better than pre-washed ones, the offer falls apart. Shelf life is UNKNOWN.
3. **Fear works against it.** "Unwashed" next to ready-to-eat salad can make shoppers think of contamination. The pain the brand names (chlorine, a "processed" smell) is mild for most buyers, while a food-safety worry is strong. The food-safety and regulatory position is UNKNOWN. One recall would end the business.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the buyer? Which shopper feels the pre-washed pain strongly enough to take on a wash step? (/founder-consumer)
- What does a container sell for, what does it cost to make, and could you double the price and still be the obvious choice? (/founder-cfo, /founder-pricing)
- How many days does it last in the fridge compared with pre-washed greens, measured rather than claimed?
- What is the guarantee? What happens if it wilts or a customer gets sick?
- Who sits next to you on the shelf (organic, hydroponic or living lettuce), and why is yours the obvious choice over them? (/founder-competitors)
- How many stores carry it, how fast does it sell and how many buyers come back for more?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Proof of the main promise:** measured shelf-life and blind taste results against pre-washed greens, printed on the pack.
2. **A guarantee the business can afford:** for example "stays fresh X days or your money back", costed by /founder-cfo.
3. **Less effort for the buyer:** shorten the wash ritual or make it feel like a reward, then test it on a panel (/founder-offer, /founder-consumer).
4. **A written, defensible food-safety position** before expanding to more stores.
5. **Repeat-purchase data** from current stores showing that buyers come back after their first wash.

# Board brief: DIRTY is Better ("Dirty Greens")

Source: the founder's one-line pitch ("unwashed leafy greens that you wash at home") plus the public website dirtyisbetter.com, read 2026-11-21. Anything marked UNKNOWN was not available; treat it as an open question, do not invent it.

## The idea
Field-packed, unwashed leafy greens sold in grocery stores. Each container includes a reusable mesh wash bag. The customer washes the greens at home instead of buying greens that were industrially pre-washed.

## What the business is today
- Stage: live. Products are on grocery shelves ("Shop in store", "Find in a store near you"). The site's testimonials include a "Gelson's Produce Manager" ("Wow. The timing of this couldn't be better.") and a "Gelson's Customer" ("The packaging is so bold and I love what the messaging was all about."). Number of stores, region and sales volume: UNKNOWN.
- Products (greens): Sir Leafs-A-Lot Arugula, Rosie's Iron Will Spinach, Kali-fornia Baby Kale, Astro Greens Mixed Leaf. Retail price: UNKNOWN (not on the site; greens are not sold online).
- Merch sold online ("DIRTY Supply Co."): tee $35, trucker hat $32, tote $30, two bandanas $20, poster set $15, koozies $10, air freshener $8.
- Grower: "Farmer Tim", described as having spent decades growing greens; the site says he noticed that "pre-washing wasn't making produce better. It was turning living food into something that smelled processed and expired before it should."
- Channels: grocery retail; Instagram and TikTok; an email list ("Get the inside dirt"); a blog ("what's on our mind").

## The pitch, in the brand's words
- Tagline: "FRESH AS F*CK. Farmers' market flavor. Grocery store convenience."
- "Dirt isn't the problem, it's proof of life."
- "DIRTY skips the chemical-treated wash and puts the rinse back in your hands."
- "Field-packed, minimally handled, and unapologetically unwashed."
- "You're funding the future of food. DIRTY is more than a bag of greens. It's a movement."

The site's comparison table:

| | Pre-washed bagged greens | DIRTY greens |
|---|---|---|
| Washed by | Shared industrial tank | You, at home |
| Rinse agent | Chlorine solution | Your sink |
| Smell | Faintly chemical | Like actual food |
| Who's in control | The factory | You |
| The vibe | Forgotten in the fridge | Actually excited about salad |

The wash ritual printed on the site: 1) open container, add water, soak 5 minutes; 2) dump water, rinse mesh bag under faucet; 3) shake bag, hang to dry; 4) eat, store leftovers in the bag in the container.

## Target customer
Not specified by the founder. Inferred from the brand only: grocery shoppers who buy bagged salad and care about freshness and less processing; the tone, merch and social channels suggest younger, style-conscious shoppers.

## Unknown (open questions for the board to flag, not fill in)
Retail price and wholesale price per container; cost of goods; shelf life versus pre-washed greens; number of stores and sell-through; acres/supply capacity and seasonality; customer acquisition cost; budget and funding; food-safety and regulatory position of selling unwashed ready-to-eat-looking greens; competitors on the same shelf.

# Round 2 update from the founder (2026-11-21)

The founder answered the first board's open questions. Take these as stated by the founder; they are not independently verified.

- **Food safety:** "top notch food safety built in and it's totally solid." The founder does not consider this an open risk.
- **Pilot:** 7 Gelson's stores in Los Angeles for 11 weeks. Strong sales and repeat sales (unit volumes and repeat rate not given).
- **Why people bought:** longer shelf life and no chemicals were the main selling features, especially during a cyclospora scare involving Taylor Farms (a large bagged-salad producer).
- **Price and margin:** retail $6.99; 35% goes to the store, so DIRTY receives about $4.54 per container.
- **Losses:** DIRTY lost $2 to $4 per container during the pilot, so the all-in cost per container was roughly $6.50 to $8.50. Cost breakdown (growing, packing labour, packaging, freight, the mesh bag, slotting) not given.
- **Scale plan that failed to fund:** the founder sought $1M to reach 60 stores, which the founder says turns a profit. The raise did not succeed. Current cash and runway: UNKNOWN.
- **The decision the board must now weigh:**
  - **Option A — scale the greens:** get more processing machinery (financed some other way than the failed equity raise) to cut unit cost and reach enough stores to profit.
  - **Option B — pivot the brand to shelf-stable:** keep the DIRTY brand, the founder's main asset, and move to shelf-stable products such as salsa or prepared meals.
  - The board may propose a hybrid or a different path if the lens points there.

# Round 3 update from the founder (2026-11-21)

The founder pushed back on round 2. Take these as stated by the founder; not independently verified. Arithmetic marked "computed" was done by the chair from the founder's numbers.

## 1. Packing cost and automation
- Today containers are packed by hand: 3 people at $25/hour pack 200 containers in 3 hours.
  - Computed: $225 per 200 containers = **about $1.13 packing labour per container**.
- A co-processor/co-packer or automation would cut that by about 70% (founder). Computed: packing falls to about $0.34, **saving about $0.79 per container**.
- Computed context: the round-2 loss was about $1.96-$3.96 per container (net $4.54 vs all-in cost $6.50-$8.50). A $0.79 packing saving alone would leave a loss of about $1.17-$3.17, unless "all-in cost" also includes other items that fall with automation or scale (e.g. shrink, freight per unit, demo costs, slotting, fixed overhead spread over few units). **The breakdown of the remaining ~$5.40-$7.40 of cost per container is not given** and is the key open number. The board should say what it needs, not assume.
- The founder also mentioned shared/scale equipment ("scared equipment" in the message; likely "shared" or "scaled") as the route to a big per-unit cost drop.

## 2. Marketing need
- A new brand that needs education; the founder says it needs "serious dollars" for marketing and social media.
- In-store demos: one demo costs $60 and reliably sells about 30 packs in 2-3 hours.
  - Computed: **about $2.00 demo cost per pack sold**, against about $4.54 net revenue per pack. Repeat rate of demo-acquired buyers: not given.

## 3. The brand is bigger than unwashed lettuce
- The founder wants the board to go deeper on the brand-led path rather than dismiss it.
- In the founder's words: DIRTY "isn't just about unwashed lettuce ... it's about harkening to a time where we all played outside and just got dirty ... fun, let our hair down." The brand is "highly edgy and multifaceted with many characters and stories."
- Characters and story assets visible on dirtyisbetter.com: Sir Leafs-A-Lot (arugula), Rosie's Iron Will (spinach), Kali-fornia Love (baby kale), Astro Greens (mixed leaf), Farmer Tim (the grower), "DIRTY Propaganda" posters, "Cool Kids Eat Dirt" tee, "Stay DIRTY" bandana, a merch line ("DIRTY Supply Co. — We started with greens and we didn't stop there"), a blog that runs a city "tour" series ("tour stop #1... Hollywood"), and the manifesto line "this revolution won't stay in the salad aisle."
- Instagram (@dirtyisbetter) could not be read from this session (Instagram refused the request), so social following and engagement are UNKNOWN.

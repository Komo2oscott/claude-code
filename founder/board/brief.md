# Board brief: DIRTY is Better ("Dirty Greens")

Source: the founder's one-line pitch ("unwashed leafy greens that you wash at home") plus the public website dirtyisbetter.com, read 2026-11-21. Anything marked UNKNOWN was not available; treat it as an open question, do not invent it.

## The idea
Field-packed, unwashed leafy greens sold in grocery stores. Each container includes a reusable mesh wash bag. The customer washes the greens at home instead of buying greens that were industrially pre-washed.

## What the business is today
- Stage: live. Products are on grocery shelves ("Shop in store", "Find in a store near you"). The site's testimonials include a "Gelson's Produce Manager" ("Wow. The timing of this couldn't be better.") and a "Gelson's Customer" ("The packaging is so bold and I love what the messaging was all about."). Number of stores, region and sales volume: UNKNOWN.
- Products (greens): Sir Leafs-A-Lot Arugula, Rosie's Iron Will Spinach, Kali-fornia Baby Kale, Astro Greens Mixed Leaf. Retail price: UNKNOWN (not on the site; greens are not sold online).
- Merch sold online ("DIRTY Supply Co."): tee $35, trucker hat $32, tote $30, two bandanas $20, poster set $15, koozies $10, air freshener $8.
- Grower: "Farmer Tim", described as having spent decades growing greens; the site says he noticed that "pre-washing wasn't making produce better. It was turning living food into something that smelled processed and expired before it should."
- Channels: grocery retail; Instagram and TikTok; an email list ("Get the inside dirt"); a blog ("what's on our mind").

## The pitch, in the brand's words
- Tagline: "FRESH AS F*CK. Farmers' market flavor. Grocery store convenience."
- "Dirt isn't the problem, it's proof of life."
- "DIRTY skips the chemical-treated wash and puts the rinse back in your hands."
- "Field-packed, minimally handled, and unapologetically unwashed."
- "You're funding the future of food. DIRTY is more than a bag of greens. It's a movement."

The site's comparison table:

| | Pre-washed bagged greens | DIRTY greens |
|---|---|---|
| Washed by | Shared industrial tank | You, at home |
| Rinse agent | Chlorine solution | Your sink |
| Smell | Faintly chemical | Like actual food |
| Who's in control | The factory | You |
| The vibe | Forgotten in the fridge | Actually excited about salad |

The wash ritual printed on the site: 1) open container, add water, soak 5 minutes; 2) dump water, rinse mesh bag under faucet; 3) shake bag, hang to dry; 4) eat, store leftovers in the bag in the container.

## Target customer
Not specified by the founder. Inferred from the brand only: grocery shoppers who buy bagged salad and care about freshness and less processing; the tone, merch and social channels suggest younger, style-conscious shoppers.

## Unknown (open questions for the board to flag, not fill in)

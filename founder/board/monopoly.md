# Monopoly lens (round 3)
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF
Recommended path: Keep greens as the proof product. Use a co-packer, not bought machinery, to fix unit cost in premium LA first. Spend on demos only where repeat buying pays them back. Extend the brand only into products where its claim can be checked.

## What changed my mind (and what didn't)
- **The arithmetic checks out.** $225 / 200 = $1.13 packing per container. A 70% cut saves about $0.79. The loss falls from $1.96-$3.96 to $1.17-$3.17. Demos cost $60 / 30 = $2.00 a pack.
- **Not changed: automation does not close the gap alone.** Packing is about $1.13 of a $6.50-$8.50 cost. The other ~$5.40-$7.40 is still unexplained, and that is where profit is won or lost.
- **Changed: a co-packer beats buying machinery.** It puts no cash into equipment, which fits a failed raise. The catch for this lens: a co-packer can pack an unwashed line for anyone. It cuts cost but builds no moat.
- **Changed: the brand is a real candidate moat.** Brand is one of the four durable advantages. Named characters, a voice and a manifesto are assets copycats cannot clone. In round 2 I treated the brand as valuable only inside greens. I now grant that a character brand can span products.
- **Not changed: the moat comes from the proof, not the posters.** "Play outside, get dirty" on its own puts DIRTY in a crowded lifestyle-brand market. What sets DIRTY apart is a claim buyers can check: it lasts longer and skips the chlorine wash. A shelf-stable replacement still drops that claim.
- **Demos:** at $2.00 of a $4.54 net, a demo-acquired first sale loses money even before product cost. Demos pay only through repeat buying, and the repeat rate is unknown.

## The brand-led path: best version and biggest risk
**Best version:** DIRTY is the fun, anti-processed fresh-food brand that owns one aisle before it moves to the next. Greens stay the flagship and the proof. Each new product has to (a) carry a "less processed" claim a buyer can check, (b) run through the same co-packer or grocery buyer, and (c) feature a character with its own story. Merch and the city tour are marketing that pays for itself, not a revenue line. In this version, marketing money builds the moat instead of being overhead.
**Biggest risk:** the brand grows faster than the business underneath. Products that lose money pay for character IP that nobody yet buys for its own sake. There is also a tension to settle. "Play outside like a kid" and characters point toward families, while "FRESH AS F*CK" points away from them. Pick the small market first.

## What would kill it (top 3)
1. **Unit cost stays above $4.54** once packing is fixed. The unexplained ~$5.40-$7.40 does not fall.
2. **A large grower or a store's private label launches an unwashed line** with the same co-packer economics, and the brand has not yet earned loyalty.
3. **The brand spreads into categories where "DIRTY" proves nothing,** and it ends up competing on vibe in crowded aisles.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Line-by-line cost of the $6.50-$8.50 (growing, packaging, mesh bag, freight, shrink, slotting, demos, overhead), and what a co-packer quote does to each. (/founder-ops, /founder-cfo)
- What share of demo buyers buy again, and within what time? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- Units per store per week in the pilot, and shelf life measured against pre-washed greens? (/founder-cfo, /founder-ops)
- Would buyers pay $7.99 or more? (/founder-pricing)
- Which character or story do buyers remember without prompting, and who is the core buyer: families or adults? (/founder-consumer, /founder-brand)
- Who could launch an unwashed line fastest? (/founder-competitors)
- Cash, runway, and financing that needs no equity: co-packer terms, equipment leases, retailer support. (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A co-packer quote and a full cost breakdown that show a cost per container below $4.54 (or a higher price) at roughly the current number of stores.
2. A measured demo repeat rate that pays back the $2.00. Until then, keep demos to the stores where they have been proven.
3. Trademark the DIRTY name and characters, sign a contracted grower supply, and print a measured shelf-life claim on the pack.
4. Brand extensions only after greens make money per unit, and only into products with a claim buyers can check. Never as a replacement for greens.

# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **There is a real secret.** The business is built on a contrarian belief: industrial pre-washing makes greens worse (they smell processed and spoil sooner), and buyers will take the wash back to win flavour and freshness. Most of the category assumes the opposite. If that belief holds up, it is the kind of insight the Monopoly lens looks for.
- **A small market it could own first.** "Unwashed greens for the home rinse" is a category of one today. Starting with a large share of a small market is the right shape.
- **Distribution has started.** The product is already on grocery shelves, with a produce manager quoted as a supporter. That means a buyer, not just a product. The store count, region and sell-through are UNKNOWN.
- **Brand is the one moat it could build.** The voice, the merch and the "movement" framing are distinctive. A brand is the only durable advantage in view.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A food-safety event, or regulation that rules out the category.** The whole position rests on selling unwashed greens that look ready to eat. One outbreak tied to a customer who skipped the rinse, or a retailer or regulator deciding the product is a liability, would end the category, not just this brand. Its regulatory standing is UNKNOWN.
2. **No proprietary edge, so a copycat arrives in month six.** Leaving out a step and adding a mesh bag is not technology. Any established grower or private-label buyer could ship "unwashed" greens next season, with more acres and a lower cost per unit. That is the incremental-improvement trap the engineering question warns about: nothing here is 10x better on anything that can be measured yet.
3. **The scale economics favour incumbents.** Bagged greens is a scale business (acres, cold chain, slotting). On scale alone, the big players win. Supply capacity, seasonality and the dependence on one grower are UNKNOWN.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What can you measure that is better? Shelf life in days versus pre-washed greens, and a blind taste preference. Is it anywhere near 10x? (/founder-consumer for preference; lab or ops data for shelf life.)
- Which named competitors share the shelf, and what stops them launching an unwashed line? (/founder-competitors)
- What is your legal and food-safety position, and what is the labelling?
- How many stores do you sell in, what is the sell-through, and what does it cost to win the next store and the first 100 repeat buyers? (/founder-cfo)
- What do the retail price, wholesale price and cost of goods leave as margin? (/founder-pricing, /founder-cfo)
- Why now? What has changed so that shoppers will accept a washing step today?
- How many acres can you supply, and what happens to supply out of season or if Farmer Tim's supply fails?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A written food-safety and labelling position that retailers accept, before expanding to more stores.
2. Measured proof of an edge, either a clear shelf-life advantage or a strong blind preference, to give the brand something real to defend.
3. Dominate one defined niche first, for example one regional premium grocer chain with high repeat purchase, before going wide.
4. A plan for durability: grower contracts or exclusive supply, plus a trademark on the "DIRTY" category language, so a copycat cannot simply take the name and the shelf.

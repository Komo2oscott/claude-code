# Product lens (round 3)
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF
Recommended path: Brand-led, with the greens as its proof: co-pack the greens to cut cost, use the characters to sell them, and extend the brand only into fresh products that fit it, after the greens stop losing money.

## What changed my mind (and what didn't)
- **Changed: the brand is bigger than the leaf.** "Play outside, get dirty, have fun" is a real point of view, and the characters (Sir Leafs-A-Lot, Rosie's Iron Will, Farmer Tim) give each product a face. In round 2 I said to pause merch. If merch pays for itself and works as advertising, it can stay.
- **Changed: demos sell.** $60 for about 30 packs is about $2.00 a pack (checked). That shows people buy once they try it.
- **Not changed: packing is not the problem.** The arithmetic checks out: 3 people x $25 x 3 hours = $225 for 200 containers, about $1.13 each. A 70% cut saves about $0.79. That still leaves a loss of about $1.17 to $3.17 a unit. Packing is only 13% to 17% of the all-in cost. About $5.40 to $7.40 a container is still unexplained, and that is where the business lives or dies.
- **Not changed: demos lose money today.** A pack sold at a demo brings in $4.54, minus $2.00 for the demo, minus a $6.50 to $8.50 cost. That is a loss of $3.96 to $5.96 on the first sale. Only repeat buying can pay that back, and each repeat sale also loses money for now.
- **Not changed: no shelf-stable salsa.** It argues against the brand's own comparison table.

## The brand-led path: best version and biggest risk
**Best version.** In one sentence: "DIRTY is food that's still alive, for people who'd rather be outside." The greens are the first and best proof of that sentence. The wash ritual is the brand's signature five minutes: soak, rinse, shake, hang, the "get dirty" moment at the sink. Each character is a product. New products come only where that sentence and ritual still hold: fresh, minimally handled, sold with the same attitude. Merch and the city tour keep the world alive between purchases. So the brand is the asset, but it borrows its credibility from a leaf people can taste.

**Biggest risk.** A lifestyle brand with "many characters and stories" and no product that makes money is a costume. More characters, products and stories pull against focus. Every new line adds cost before the first one works. There is a second risk too: playful "dirt" right after a cyclospora scare can make some buyers and produce buyers doubt food safety, the very thing the founder calls solid.

## What would kill it (top 3)
1. **The unexplained $5.40-$7.40 does not fall with co-packing or scale.** Then every marketing dollar buys more losses. (/founder-ops, /founder-cfo)
2. **Repeat buying is low or depended on the scare.** Then the $2.00 demo cost is never earned back. (/founder-consumer)
3. **The brand spreads before the hero product works.** Characters, merch and extensions use up cash the greens need. (/founder-brand, /founder-marketing)

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Line by line, what is the cost per container: leaf, packaging, mesh bag, freight, shrink, slotting, demos, overhead? Which lines does co-packing cut? (/founder-ops, /founder-cfo)
- What co-packer per-unit quote and minimum order can you get, with no equipment to buy? (/founder-ops)
- What share of demo buyers buy again, and how soon? (/founder-consumer, /founder-marketing)
- Do merch and social cover their own cost? What is the following? (/founder-marketing)
- Can the price move above $6.99 against the shelf? (/founder-pricing, /founder-competitors)
- What cash and runway do you have now? (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cost first:** a full cost breakdown and a signed co-packer quote showing a cost below $4.54 per container. Prefer a per-unit co-packer contract to buying machinery, because it needs no equity raise.
2. **Money split:** most of the scarce cash goes to closing the cost gap. Demos run as a measured test, held to a budget, tracking repeat buying in the 7 Gelson's stores. Paid social waits until each unit makes money.
3. **Focus:** lead with the best-selling one or two characters. Merch stays only if it breaks even.
4. **Extensions:** fresh, brand-true products only, and only after the greens break even per unit.

# Product lens (round 2)
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF
Recommended path: A, narrowed. The fresh, unwashed leaf is the one thing the brand stands for; fix the cost of that one thing before adding stores, and do not pivot away from it.

## What is strong
- **The pilot says the experience works.** Buyers named longer shelf life and no chemicals as the reasons they bought. Those are the product's own promises, not the merch or the attitude. The founder also reports repeat buying.
- **Still one sentence:** "unwashed greens that last longer, washed at home." Option B has no sentence like that yet.
- **The brand is earned by the leaf.** "Dirt is proof of life" and "Fresh as f*ck" only mean something on a living product. That is why the brand is worth keeping.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Every unit loses money, and more stores multiply the loss.** DIRTY gets $4.54 from each $6.99 container and spends about $6.50 to $8.50 on it, so it loses $1.96 to $3.96 a unit. Cost has to fall 30% to 47% to break even. The plan to reach 60 stores comes before the cost fix, so it scales the loss. Nobody has shown which parts of cost machinery removes. The mesh bag in every container is a cost the customer only needs once.
2. **Option B throws away the one thing.** A shelf-stable salsa or prepared meal has no wash ritual, no "proof of life" and no shelf-life edge over its neighbours. The brand's whole argument is against processed food that sits on a shelf. B would ask buyers to trust "DIRTY" on the kind of product its own comparison table mocks, in a new category where the business has no pilot and no proof.
3. **Demand may have been borrowed from a scare.** The pilot ran during a cyclospora scare at a large bagged-salad producer. If "no shared wash tank" mattered most because of that news, the reason to buy fades when the news does, and repeat buying is the only test of that.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which of your cost items does machinery cut, by how many cents each, and is the cut 30% or more? (/founder-ops, /founder-cfo)
- What does the mesh bag cost per container? What would bag-once-then-refill do to cost and to the ritual? (/founder-ops, /founder-cfo)
- What were unit sales and repeat rate week by week, before, during and after the scare? (/founder-consumer can test whether the reason holds without the news.)
- Which one of the four products sold best? Could one product carry the brand for now?
- Can $6.99 move up, given what sits next to it on the shelf? (/founder-pricing, /founder-competitors)
- What do your current cash and runway allow? (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cost before stores:** a cost breakdown per container showing a path to below $4.54, achieved or contracted, before any expansion past the pilot stores. Machinery financing (equipment loan, lease or a retailer or co-packer partner) only once that breakdown shows machinery is what closes the gap.
2. **Cut to the core:** the best one or two products, the bag sold once with refills after (or a cost case for keeping it), and merch paused.
3. **Repeat proof outside the scare:** repeat rate in the 7 Gelson's stores over a period with no food-safety headlines.
4. **No shelf-stable launch** until the greens break even per unit. Line extensions come from a brand that has earned them on its core product, not instead of one.

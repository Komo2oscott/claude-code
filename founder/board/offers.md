# Offers lens (round 3)
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF
Recommended path: brand-led, greens first. Make each container earn money (co-packer plus price test), fund demos only where repeat buys pay them back, and cheaply test brand pull before any second product.

## What changed my mind (and what didn't)
- **The arithmetic checks out.** $225 / 200 = $1.13 packing; minus 70% = $0.34, saving $0.79. Net $4.54 ($6.99 x 0.65) against $6.50-$8.50 leaves a loss of $1.96-$3.96, or $1.17-$3.17 after the saving. Packing covers only 20-40% of the gap.
- **Changed:** a co-packer is better than buying machines. It turns a fixed cost into a per-unit cost and needs no equity.
- **Changed:** marketing is a real need, and demos are a good offer tool. At $2.00 a pack they are cheap and measurable. But today a demo-sold pack loses $3.96-$5.96 on the first sale. Until each container earns money, marketing enlarges the loss.
- **Changed, partly:** the brand can be the asset that spans products. Shelf-stable is no longer an automatic no.
- **Not changed:** pilot buyers said they bought for shelf life and no chemicals. Those are product reasons; no evidence yet of buying the brand itself (merch sales and social reach UNKNOWN).

## The brand-led path: best version and biggest risk
**Best version:** "DIRTY" stands for food that is less processed, less handled and more alive, sold with fun and characters. Every product carries a *true product claim* that matches the name (no chemical wash, short ingredient list), so the brand adds to a real benefit rather than replacing one. The characters make the product easier to learn about and to remember. Merch and the city tours test whether people want the brand itself.
**Biggest risk:** a lifestyle brand sells identity, not relief from a pain. It needs reach that is expensive to buy, bought here while losing money on every unit. A salsa called DIRTY without a dirty-specific benefit is just another salsa with fun packaging.

## What would kill it (top 3)
1. Each container still loses money after the co-packer and price changes, and the remaining ~$5.40-$7.40 of cost never falls. (/founder-cfo, /founder-ops)
2. Demo buyers do not come back, so $2.00 buys one losing sale. (/founder-consumer)
3. The brand pulls no one on its own: merch and social stay flat. (/founder-brand, /founder-competitors)

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Line by line, what is in the $6.50-$8.50, and which lines fall with a co-packer or scale? (/founder-cfo)
- What is a co-packer's per-unit quote, minimum order and terms? (/founder-ops)
- What share of demo buyers buy again within 30 days? (/founder-consumer)
- What do sales do at $8.99 or $9.99? (/founder-pricing)
- What are merch revenue and margin, social reach, and current cash? (/founder-marketing, /founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A cost model with a co-packer quote that shows money earned per container at a tested price. (/founder-cfo, /founder-pricing)
2. Split the money roughly in this order: unit cost first, then demos, but only in stores where demo buyers come back enough to pay back the $2.00. Social spend comes last. (/founder-marketing)
3. Measure brand pull (merch sell-through, list growth) before funding shelf-stable. (/founder-brand)
4. Any extension must carry a true "DIRTY" product claim and launch only after greens earn money per container.
5. Financing: co-packer terms or debt secured on orders, sized to the model in condition 1, not a fresh $1M equity raise. (/founder-cfo)

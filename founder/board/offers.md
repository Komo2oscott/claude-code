# Offers lens (round 2)
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF
Recommended path: hybrid. Keep the greens, because the offer's value lives in them, and fix the price and the proof in the 7 current stores before borrowing for machinery. Shelf-stable is a different offer in a weaker position.

## What is strong
- **The pain turned out to be real.** Shoppers bought for longer shelf life and no chemicals, not for the wash ritual. Those two are dream-outcome and likelihood drivers, and they are much stronger than the "chlorine smell" pain I worried about in round 1.
- **Repeat sales across 11 weeks in 7 stores** suggest the extra effort of washing did not drive buyers away after the first try. That was my top kill risk in round 1, and it now looks smaller (repeat rate not given).
- **It is not competing on price.** At $6.99 it sells as a premium choice and people still bought it.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The offer is priced below its cost.** DIRTY nets $4.54 against an all-in cost of $6.50 to $8.50. Breaking even at today's price needs a 30% to 47% cost cut, and machinery is an unproven bet on that. Alternatively, $10.00 retail covers the low cost estimate and about $13.08 covers the high one (same 35% store share). Nobody has tested whether buyers would pay that.
2. **Demand may have been borrowed from a scare.** Sales were strongest during a competitor's cyclospora scare. Urgency borrowed from someone else's crisis fades.
3. **Option B throws away the offer.** "Unwashed, longer-lasting, no chemical wash" means nothing for salsa or a prepared meal. B keeps the name but none of the reasons the pilot buyers gave. It starts with no proof and no clear edge (competitors unknown: /founder-competitors).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many units did each store sell per week, and what share of buyers came back? How did that change after the scare faded? (/founder-consumer)
- What makes up the $6.50 to $8.50, and which line does machinery cut, by how much? (/founder-cfo, /founder-ops)
- What happens to sales at $8.99, $9.99 or $11.99? (/founder-pricing)
- How many extra days of shelf life do you measure against pre-washed greens?
- Who sits next to DIRTY on the shelf at Gelson's, and at what price? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Price test before any spend on machinery:** run a higher price (for example $8.99 to $9.99) in some of the 7 stores and track repeat purchases. (/founder-pricing)
2. **Put the proof on the pack:** a measured shelf-life claim plus a "fresh for X days or your money back" guarantee, costed by /founder-cfo. (/founder-offer)
3. **A unit-cost model that reaches break-even** at the tested price and at a store count the business can reach without the failed $1M raise. Finance the machinery with debt or the supplier only if that model holds. (/founder-cfo, /founder-ops)
4. **Repeat-rate data from the weeks after the scare**, showing demand stays up without a competitor's crisis behind it.
5. **Keep shelf-stable as a later brand extension**, to try only once the greens make money per container, and never as a replacement for them.
